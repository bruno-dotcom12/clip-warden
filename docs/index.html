Clip Warden

An open-source agent that cuts long videos into vertical clips for paid clipping campaigns, and keeps each clip inside the campaign's rules.

What it does

Paid clipping campaigns reject submissions after the views have already accrued, and a rejected clip is unpaid work. The reasons are almost always mechanical: a second over the limit, a missing hashtag, footage that did not come from the archive the brief authorises.

You give Clip Warden a campaign link and a source video. It reads the brief, writes down what the campaign demands, transcribes the video, picks the moments that stand on their own, renders vertical clips that already sit inside the rules, and hands the files back with a caption you can paste.

Where it runs

Clip Warden runs on your own computer, inside a container you start yourself. There is no Clip Warden server, no account to create, and no hosted service. Your videos are read from your disk and written back to your disk. Nothing is uploaded to us, because there is no "us" to upload to.

Publishing to social platforms

Publishing is optional. Clip Warden works fully without connecting any social account, and it never connects one on its own. When you choose to connect an account, this is exactly what happens:

  • TikTok. With your permission (scope video.upload), Clip Warden sends a finished clip to the drafts inbox of your own TikTok account. It does not publish. You open TikTok, review the draft, edit it if you want, choose who can see it, and post it yourself.
  • Instagram. With your permission, Clip Warden can publish a Reel to your own Instagram professional account, using the caption you approved beforehand.

Clip Warden only ever touches accounts you connected yourself. It cannot post to anyone else's account, and it does not read your feed, your followers, your messages, or your analytics.

What it does not promise

It does not promise views, payment, or that a campaign will accept your clip. A human still decides whether a moment is worth clipping, and a campaign can reject a submission for editorial reasons no software can predict. What Clip Warden checks is the mechanical part: duration, resolution, required hashtags, audio rules, and the source of the footage.
